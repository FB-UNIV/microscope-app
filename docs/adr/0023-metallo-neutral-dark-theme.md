# The app is Métallo, in neutral greys with one copper accent

Until now the app had no name of its own ("Microscope Capture"), no icon, and the platform's default look, with a few inline colours. It is named **Métallo**: the window title, the icon, the About box and the Fiche PDF's creator say so. The exe, the zips, the repo and the config folder keep their names, so nothing a lab PC already has moves. `--version` prints `Metallo v…` without the accent: a Windows console would garble the é. The fallback Vue folder, used only when there is no config folder, stays `Microscope Capture/vues`, where Vues may already be.

The look is a dark theme built on Fusion (`app/theme.cpp`), decided for what students do with it: judge the colours and contrast of a photo. The greys around a photo are neutral, so they tint nothing, and dark, so the photo is the brightest thing on screen, as in other imaging tools. The one accent is copper, a nod to metallurgy, used for the primary action, the active tool and the selection. It is kept apart from the colours drawn on photos: red measurement lines, yellow handles and labels, the cyan Zone. Considered a light theme: the black live view sits like a hole in a white window, and white chrome around a micrograph shifts how bright it looks. Considered following the Windows setting: two palettes to keep and to check on every lab PC, for no gain at the microscope. Considered blue or teal accents: too close to the cyan Zone.

The icon is an eyepiece's field of view on an etched steel: ferrite grains lit copper, one dark pearlite grain. `app/resources/metallo.svg` is its source (`metallo-small.svg` for 16 to 24 px); the app embeds PNGs and the exe an `.ico` drawn from them, because loading SVG needs the Qt Svg module, which neither CI job installs. The style sheet's chevrons and check mark are PNGs for the same reason.

**Consequences:** Widgets get their look from properties the style sheet reads (`variant="primary"`, `callout="warning"`, `role="muted"`…), set with `theme::setRole()`, instead of inline colours. Widgets that paint themselves (preview, image view, histogram, Vue list) take their colours from `theme.hpp`. Title bars are dark from Qt 6.8 (the Windows bundle); the Linux CI job's Qt 6.4 leaves them to the system. On-screen text is kept short: no ADR numbers, file names or issue numbers in the UI; the reasons live here.
