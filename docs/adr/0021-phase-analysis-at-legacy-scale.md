# Phase analysis runs at the legacy camera's scale; a missing Seuil is reported, never guessed

**Status:** amends [ADR 0012](0012-legacy-measurements-ported-bit-exact.md) for images wider than the legacy frame; on legacy-sized images the port stays bit-exact.

Trying the app on the lab's 4K frames, CALCUL on the whole photo gave 59.6 % dark phase with a 40 % "uncertainty" and an empty histogram, while a zone of the same photo, or the same sample photographed at 1080p, gave about 20 %. The legacy auto-threshold finds edges by comparing each pixel with its neighbours, squared and divided by 512 — tuned on the old camera's 1042×780 frames. The Moticam A5's 4K frames are soft at the pixel level (`docs/hardware/camera-probe.md`), so neighbouring pixels barely differ, no gradient bin stands out, the edge-weighted histogram comes out empty, and the legacy code falls back to its default Seuil of 127 ± 42 without saying so. The darker the exposure, the worse.

Decided: `analyzeVuePhases()` scales an image wider than 1042 px down to that width (area averaging) before the legacy pipeline, and a zone by the same factor as its frame, so the algorithm sees edges as sharp as on the old camera. An image no wider is analysed as it is, so legacy parity (the in-repo crops, the archived CTTM TIFFs) is unchanged. `AutoThreshold` gains `found`, false when the histogram is empty; the app then says it found no Seuil instead of printing the defaults and a meaningless percentage. On the lab frame `r2-exp9` the whole photo now gives 21.8 % dark, Seuil 94 ± 4, in line with its zones and with the 1080p shot of the same sample.

Considered changing the gradient to compare pixels further apart at high resolution. Rejected: it changes the legacy algorithm itself, where scaling only changes what it is fed, and area-averaging also brings the legacy 5×5 smoothing back to the physical size it had on the old camera. Considered capturing at a lower resolution. Rejected: Calibrations are tied to the 4K frame (ADR 0015) and the Fiche prints from it.

**Consequences:** Percentages on 4K Vues are computed on a ≈1042 px wide copy; the phase map is still drawn on the full photo with the Seuil found. A zone must be at least 5×5 pixels at that scale (19×19 on a 4K frame) — the size of the legacy smoothing disk — and there is no other size rule (#55, #64). Results on 4K differ slightly with the scale chosen, as they do between two photos of the same sample; they no longer collapse to the default Seuil.
