# Calibrate one objective on the mire; derive the others from the Grossissement

**Status:** amends the one-Calibration-per-objective model of CONTEXT.md and #22, and the consequence of [ADR 0015](0015-calibration-tied-to-frame-size.md) that a new camera mode means recalibrating every objective. ADR 0015's frame-size check still stands, for derived Calibrations too.

At the first lab session (2026-10-16) only x5 could be calibrated: there was no mire at hand for the other objectives, so every x10, x20 or x50 Vue came out without an Échelle, Measurement or Dureté Vickers. The teacher pointed out that the legacy app used one calibration for all objectives, with the Grossissement as a coefficient. The decompile agrees: one sensor-plane constant, 288.65 px/mm at 1×, multiplied by G (`docs/legacy/reverse-engineering-report.md`). It works because the camera and its adapter are the same for every objective, so px/µm scales with the objective alone.

Decided: calibrating any objective on the mire is enough. An objective with no mire Calibration of its own gets one **derived** from the reference: px/µm × G ÷ G_ref, where G is the first number in the Grossissement's name ("x10" → 10). The reference is the objective measured on the mire most recently. An objective that has its own mire Calibration always uses it. A derived Calibration keeps the reference's frame size (ADR 0015 applies unchanged) and records `derived_from`, so a Vue's sidecar says where its scale came from.

Trade-off: an objective's real magnification can differ from the engraved one by a few percent. A length measured with a derived Calibration is off by that much, and a Vickers hardness (1/d²) by about twice as much. Measuring that objective on the mire removes the error, and the app still lets you do it. Considered keeping one Calibration per objective only. Rejected: without a mire for every objective, the alternative is no scale at all, which is worse than a few percent. Considered averaging every measured objective into one constant. Rejected: one bad reading would shift every objective and nobody could tell which.

**Consequences:** `Calibration` gains `derivedFrom` (`"derived_from"` in JSON, written only for a derived one, so existing files keep their layout). `WorkstationConfig` stores only mire Calibrations, refuses a derived one, and gains `referenceGrossissement` (`"reference_grossissement"`). A file without it, or naming no Calibration, derives from the last one listed. `effectiveCalibration()` returns the measured or derived Calibration, and `calibrationForFrame()` goes through it, so capture, the Échelle and Measurement get derived Calibrations with no change of their own. A Grossissement whose name has no number (e.g. "Macro") stays uncalibrated unless measured. Recalibrating the reference changes every derived objective's future Vues; past Vues keep their own copy, as before. `tools/make_mire.py` draws a synthetic mire with a known pitch, to try all this without a microscope.
